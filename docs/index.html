Doorman

Est. on an M5StickC

A Wi-Fi doorman for devices that can't sign themselves in.

Connect Alexa, Echo, smart bulbs, printers and consoles to hotel, hostel or campus Wi-Fi that demands a captive portal login or WPA2-Enterprise.

Your smart bulb doesn't have a browser. It will never get past the login page your Wi-Fi shows you. There's no setting you can change, because the device physically cannot complete that login.

Install it from this page

Plug an M5StickC into this computer with a USB-C cable, then click Install. Nothing to download and no toolchain to set up.

Needs Chrome or Edge on a desktop, since Safari and Firefox don't support Web Serial. Other browsers can install manually.

Then set it up

  • Join the Doorman Wi-Fi network. Default password is changeme123.
  • Open 192.168.4.1 and enter your Wi-Fi details. Change that default password while you're there.
  • Save. It reboots and connects.
  • If your network has a captive portal, open any page and log in once.
  • Set your smart devices up against the Doorman network.

How it works

Doorman joins the awkward network as a normal client, then re-broadcasts it as a plain WPA2-PSK network with NAT. Your devices join that one instead.

Wi-Fi with a captive portal / WPA2-Enterprise | [ Doorman ] | NAT, so everything behind here | shares ONE upstream MAC | private WPA2-PSK network, no portal | +-----------+-----------+ | | | bulb speaker printer

Because everything leaves through one MAC address, one login covers every device behind it. Sign in once from your phone and your bulb, speaker and printer come online with you.

What it isn't

Doorman is a NAT router. It's the same job your home router does, and the same job your phone does when you switch on a personal hotspot.

It doesn't crack or evade authentication, doesn't defeat the portal (a person logs in through it, normally), doesn't spoof MAC addresses, and doesn't disguise traffic. Your network sees one completely ordinary client. The only thing that changes is how many of your devices fit behind a single login.

Before you deploy it

Read your network's acceptable use policy. Some cap devices per account or forbid running an access point, and breaking that usually costs you the account. Don't put this on an employer's or university's network: an unregistered access point there is treated as a security incident. Ask whoever runs it instead, since most have a process for registering devices.

Honest limits

  • Measured around 4 Mbps on a congested 2.4 GHz channel, where a laptop got 33 Mbps on the same network's 5 GHz band. The ESP32 has no 5 GHz radio, and one radio serves both networks at once, so every forwarded packet uses the same channel twice. Fine for bulbs and speakers, which is the point. Not fine for your laptop.
  • One radio, shared. When the upstream roams you to another access point, everything behind Doorman drops for a few seconds. That's hardware, not a bug.
  • 2.4 GHz only. The ESP32 has no 5 GHz radio.
  • Keep it on USB power. The internal battery is 200 mAh.
